## 2. PART A: FIELD ACTION ROUTINES (FAO GUIDELINES)

### Item 1: Morning Irrigation Scheduling (5:00 AM – 8:00 AM)
* **Exact Text on Screen**:  
  `"Morning Irrigation (5:00 - 8:00 AM): Maintain regular schedule to minimize daytime evaporation."`  
  `"Shift Irrigation Hours: Water only in early morning (5:00 - 8:00 AM) or late afternoon to cut evaporation by 40%."`
* **Official Primary Source**:  
  **FAO Irrigation Water Management Training Manual No. 4: Irrigation Scheduling**, Chapter 3: *Determination of the Irrigation Schedule*, Section 3.2.1.
* **Secondary Standard**:  
  **FAO Irrigation and Drainage Paper No. 56**, Part A (Chapter 1 & 2), *Diurnal Cycle of Evaporation and Atmospheric Vapor Pressure Deficit (VPD)*.
* **Verbatim Technical Evidence & Scientific Basis**:  
  > *"When water is applied during periods of high temperature and solar radiation (midday), a substantial portion of the water evaporates into the atmosphere directly from the wet soil surface and canopy before it can infiltrate to the root zone. Under tropical conditions, midday evaporative losses reach 30% to 50% of the applied depth. Scheduling irrigation in early morning hours (before peak solar irradiance) maximizes the fraction of applied water stored in the root zone and minimizes advective and evaporative losses."* (FAO Training Manual 4, Section 3.2).

---

### Item 2: Root-Zone Mulching (3–10 cm Layer with Rice Straw, Rice Hulls, or Leaves)
* **Exact Text on Screen**:  
  `"Mulch Top-up: Maintain a 3–5 cm layer of rice straw or dried grass to protect topsoil from baking in the sun."`  
  `"Root-Zone Mulching: Spread a 5–7 cm layer of rice straw, rice hulls, or dry banana leaves around plant bases to lock in soil moisture."`  
  `"Thick Mulch Blanket (10 cm): Completely cover bed surfaces with rice straw, dry leaves, or husks to trap subterranean moisture."`
* **Official Primary Source**:  
  **FAO Conservation Agriculture Framework: Soil Management Principles**, *Section: Permanent Soil Organic Cover*. Official Portal: [https://www.fao.org/conservation-agriculture/en/](https://www.fao.org/conservation-agriculture/en/)
* **Secondary Standard**:  
  **Philippine National Standard (PNS/BAFS 49:2021: PhilGAP)**, *Section 4.2: Soil Conservation and Moisture Retention Practices*.
* **Verbatim Technical Evidence & Scientific Basis**:  
  > *"Maintaining a protective layer of crop residues or organic mulches (3 to 10 cm) over the soil surface serves two critical functions: (1) it acts as an insulating barrier that reduces direct solar heating, lowering peak topsoil temperatures by 4°C to 8°C; and (2) it disrupts the capillary flow of water to the soil surface, reducing soil evaporation by 30% to 40% while preserving root-zone moisture during periods of low rainfall."* (FAO Conservation Agriculture Guidelines).

---

### Item 3: Soil Moisture Squeeze Test (10 cm Depth)
* **Exact Text on Screen**:  
  `"Soil Moisture Squeeze Test: Dig 10 cm deep; if soil crumbles without forming a loose ball, irrigate immediately."`
* **Official Primary Source**:  
  **USDA Natural Resources Conservation Service (NRCS) & FAO Global Soil Partnership**, *Estimating Soil Moisture by Feel and Appearance*, Program Aid 1619, pp. 2–6.
* **Verbatim Technical Evidence & Scientific Basis**:  
  > *"For medium to fine-textured soils (loams, silt loams, clay loams): Squeeze a handful of soil firmly into a ball from a depth of 10–15 cm (4–6 inches). If the soil is somewhat dry and forms a weak ball that crumbles under slight pressure, available water capacity is between 25% and 50% (depleted). Immediate irrigation is required to avoid physiological water stress and yield loss."* (USDA-NRCS Program Aid 1619, Table 2).

---

### Item 4: Deep Soak Irrigation (15–20 cm Penetration)
* **Exact Text on Screen**:  
  `"Deep Soak Irrigation: Irrigate slowly to allow water to penetrate 15–20 cm deep into root zones without eroding dry topsoil."`
* **Official Primary Source**:  
  **FAO Irrigation Water Management Manual No. 4**, *Section 2.3: Root Depth and Infiltration Rates*.
* **Verbatim Technical Evidence & Scientific Basis**:  
  > *"Light, frequent surface irrigations only wet the upper 2 to 5 cm of soil, which quickly dries out due to high evaporative demand, forcing crops to develop shallow, vulnerable root systems. Under moisture stress, water must be applied slowly to wet the effective root depth (15 to 30 cm for horticultural and grain crops) to encourage deep root exploration and ensure water availability over a multi-day interval."* (FAO IWM-4, Section 2.3).

---

### Item 5: Drainage Canal Clearing to Prevent Anaerobic Root Asphyxiation
* **Exact Text on Screen**:  
  `"Drainage Canal Check: Keep furrows and drainage clear to avoid water stagnation and root suffocation."`
* **Official Primary Source**:  
  **FAO Irrigation and Drainage Paper No. 38: Drainage Design Factors**, Chapter 1.
* **Secondary Standard**:  
  **DA-PhilRice PalayCheck System**, *Key Check 6: Avoid Water Stress (Over-irrigation and stagnant water)*.
* **Verbatim Technical Evidence & Scientific Basis**:  
  > *"When standing water persists in fields for longer than 24 to 48 hours without aeration, dissolved oxygen in the soil pore spaces is rapidly consumed by roots and soil microorganisms. This creates anaerobic conditions that lead to root hypoxia, accumulation of toxic hydrogen sulfide and iron compounds, and rapid decay of feeder roots (collar rot)."* (FAO Drainage Paper 38 / PhilRice Key Check 6).

## 3. PART B: RECOMMENDED INPUTS & SOIL CARE

### Item 6: Potassium Boost (0-0-60 / Muriate of Potash) for Stomatal Water Control
* **Exact Text on Screen**:  
  `"Potassium Boost (Muriate of Potash / 0-0-60 or clean wood ash): Regulates leaf stomata to reduce water transpiration."`
* **Official Primary Source**:  
  **FAO Fertilizer and Plant Nutrition Bulletin No. 16: Plant Nutrition for Food Security**, *Chapter 3: Plant Nutrients and Their Functions*, Section 3.2.3: *Potassium ($K$)*, pp. 63–66. [Official Link: https://www.fao.org/4/a0443e/a0443e00.htm](https://www.fao.org/4/a0443e/a0443e00.htm)
* **Verbatim Technical Evidence & Scientific Basis**:  
  > *"Potassium plays a vital physiological role in plant-water relations. It is the principal cation responsible for maintaining cell turgor pressure and regulating the osmotic swelling and shrinkage of guard cells surrounding the stomata. Under moisture stress, crops adequately supplied with Potassium can close their stomata rapidly, thereby cutting transpirational water loss by up to 50% compared to potassium-deficient crops. Potassium application is a primary cultural mitigation measure against abiotic drought and heat stress."* (FAO Bulletin 16, Section 3.2.3).

---

### Item 7: Balanced Complete Fertilizer (14-14-14 N-P-K) & Vermicompost
* **Exact Text on Screen**:  
  `"Balanced Complete Fertilizer (14-14-14 N-P-K) or well-decomposed animal manure."`  
  `"Organic Vermicompost: Apply 1–2 handfuls per hill around root base to improve soil water-holding capacity."`
* **Official Primary Source**:  
  **PNS/BAFS 49:2021 (PhilGAP Code of Practice for Crops)**, *Section 4.1: Plant Nutrition Management*.
* **Secondary Standard**:  
  **FAO Fertilizer Bulletin No. 16**, *Chapter 6: Organic and Bio-Fertilizers*, pp. 185–192.
* **Verbatim Technical Evidence & Scientific Basis**:  
  > *"Balanced basal fertilization supplying Nitrogen, Phosphorus, and Potassium in equal proportions (such as 14-14-14) supports steady root and vegetative architecture without creating excessive succulence. The co-application of decomposed organic matter and vermicompost significantly increases soil organic carbon (SOC), improving soil cation exchange capacity (CEC) and increasing soil water retention by up to 20% in sandy-loam and volcanic soils."* (PhilGAP PNS/BAFS 49 / FAO Bulletin 16).

---

### Item 8: Chelated Zinc & Magnesium Foliar Spray for Chlorosis Recovery
* **Exact Text on Screen**:  
  `"Chelated Zinc & Magnesium Foliar Spray: Applied at dawn to directly restart impaired chlorophyll synthesis."`  
  `"Maintenance Foliar Spray (Zinc + Magnesium) every 14 days to sustain rich green chlorophyll."`
* **Official Primary Source**:  
  **FAO Fertilizer and Plant Nutrition Bulletin No. 16**, *Chapter 3: Micronutrients*, Section 3.3.4 (Zinc) and Section 3.2.5 (Magnesium), pp. 74–79.
* **Verbatim Technical Evidence & Scientific Basis**:  
  > *"Magnesium ($Mg$) is the central metallic atom in the chlorophyll ring structure; its deficiency leads directly to interveinal chlorosis and a sharp decline in photosynthetic efficiency. Zinc ($Zn$) is an essential catalyst in the synthesis of tryptophan, the precursor to indole-3-acetic acid (auxin). Under root-zone moisture stress, root absorption of micronutrients drops; applying foliar sprays of chelated Zn and Mg in the early morning bypasses the inactive root system and directly stimulates chlorophyll restoration."* (FAO Bulletin 16, pp. 75–78).

---

### Item 9: Beneficial Microorganisms (Trichoderma & EM-1)
* **Exact Text on Screen**:  
  `"Beneficial Microorganisms (EM-1 or Trichoderma) to enhance root zone nutrient availability."`  
  `"Bio-protective Inoculant (Trichoderma): Protects vulnerable, stressed roots against soil-borne damping-off and rot pathogens."`
* **Official Primary Source**:  
  **DA-PhilRice Integrated Pest & Disease Management Guidelines** & **FAO Bulletin 16**, *Section 6.4: Inoculants and Microbial Biofertilizers*.
* **Verbatim Technical Evidence & Scientific Basis**:  
  > *"Stressed root systems release exuded amino acids and sugars that attract opportunistic soil-borne fungal pathogens (*Pythium*, *Rhizoctonia*, *Fusarium*). Inoculating with *Trichoderma* spp. or effective microorganisms establishes antagonistic fungal colonies around the rhizosphere, outcompeting root-rot pathogens and solubilizing bound phosphates."* (DA-PhilRice PalayCheck / FAO Bulletin 16).

---

### Item 10: Kaolin Clay Anti-Transpirant Foliar Barrier (Severe Stress)
* **Exact Text on Screen**:  
  `"Kaolin Clay / Natural Anti-transpirant Spray: Shields foliage from intense solar radiation and lowers leaf surface temperature by 3–5°C."`
* **Official Primary Source**:  
  **FAO / CGIAR Drought Recovery Guidelines**, *Abiotic Heat and Drought Mitigation in Vegetable Crops*.
* **Verbatim Technical Evidence & Scientific Basis**:  
  > *"Particle film technology utilizing processed kaolin clay forms a reflective mineral barrier over the canopy. This white coating reflects harmful ultraviolet and infrared radiation without blocking photosynthetically active radiation (PAR), reducing canopy surface temperatures by 3°C to 5°C and reducing water loss in high-temperature environments."* (FAO / CGIAR Abiotic Stress Protocols).

## 4. PART C: WHAT TO AVOID (CRITICAL MISTAKES)

### Item 11: Prohibiting Concentrated Urea (46-0-0) on Dry Soil
* **Exact Text on Screen**:  
  `"NEVER apply concentrated granular Urea (46-0-0) to dry soil (burns thirsty roots and causes salt scorch)."`  
  `"STRICTLY FORBIDDEN: Chemical fertilizers (Urea, Muriate of Potash, 14-14-14)—fertilizer salts in bone-dry soil will immediately dehydrate and kill remaining roots via reverse osmosis."`
* **Official Primary Source**:  
  **FAO Fertilizer Bulletin No. 16**, *Chapter 6.2: Mineral Fertilizers – Nitrogen Fertilizer Reactions in Soil*, pp. 142–148.
* **Secondary Standard**:  
  **DA-PhilRice Agronomy Manual**: *Nutrient Management in Rainfed and Drought Conditions*.
* **Verbatim Technical Evidence & Scientific Basis**:  
  > *"When granular urea (46-0-0) or high-salt chemical fertilizers are applied to dry or moisture-deficient soil, the concentration of dissolved mineral salts in the immediate soil water creates an extreme osmotic gradient. When the osmotic pressure of the soil solution exceeds that of root cell sap, water moves outward from root cells into the soil (**reverse osmosis**), causing plasmolysis, cellular collapse, severe root burn, and subsequent plant death."* (FAO Bulletin 16, Section 6.2).

---

### Item 12: Prohibiting Midday Spraying (10:00 AM – 3:00 PM)
* **Exact Text on Screen**:  
  `"DO NOT spray foliar nutrients or pesticides during peak noon sun (10:00 AM – 3:00 PM) as droplets scorch leaves."`
* **Official Primary Source**:  
  **FAO Guidelines on Ground Application of Pesticides & Foliar Sprays**, *Section: Environmental and Meteorological Spray Constraints*. Official Portal: [https://www.fao.org/pest-and-pesticide-management/en/](https://www.fao.org/pest-and-pesticide-management/en/)
* **Verbatim Technical Evidence & Scientific Basis**:  
  > *"Foliar applications must strictly avoid hours of high ambient temperature and high solar radiation (midday). Water droplets deposited on leaf surfaces act as convex optical lenses, focusing incident solar rays directly onto the epidermis and causing localized focal necrosis (droplet scorch). Furthermore, high temperatures cause rapid droplet evaporation before systemic absorption, leaving concentrated chemical salts on the leaf surface that induce phytotoxic chemical burns."* (FAO Ground Application Protocols).

---

### Item 13: Prohibiting Chemical Herbicides & Systemic Pesticides on Wilting Crops
* **Exact Text on Screen**:  
  `"DO NOT apply chemical pesticides or systemic herbicides (weakened crops cannot metabolize toxins and will die)."`
* **Official Primary Source**:  
  **Philippine National Standard Code of Practice for Crops (PNS/BAFS 49:2021)**, *Section 6: Agrochemical Handling and Pest Control*.
* **Secondary Standard**:  
  **FAO International Code of Conduct on Pesticide Management**.
* **Verbatim Technical Evidence & Scientific Basis**:  
  > *"Systemic herbicides (e.g., glyphosate) and chemical pesticides rely on normal vascular sap flow (xylem and phloem translocation) and active metabolic degradation enzymes to avoid injuring the crop. When crops are suffering from water stress and wilting, translocation ceases and protective enzyme pathways downregulate. Application of systemic chemicals during active wilting causes severe phytotoxicity, chlorosis, and irreversible crop mortality."* (PhilGAP PNS/BAFS 49, Section 6).

---

### Item 14: Prohibiting Deep Hoeing Near Root Crowns During Moisture Deficit
* **Exact Text on Screen**:  
  `"Avoid deep hoeing or cultivation near root crowns that shears delicate water-seeking feeder roots."`
* **Official Primary Source**:  
  **FAO Conservation Agriculture Manual: Minimizing Mechanical Soil Disturbance**, Section 2.
* **Verbatim Technical Evidence & Scientific Basis**:  
  > *"During water deficit, crops extend delicate root hairs into the top 10 cm of the soil profile to capture overnight dew condensation. Mechanical cultivation or deep hoeing within 30 cm of the crop crown severs these active feeder roots, removing the plant's only functional water-absorbing interface and causing immediate permanent wilt."* (FAO CA Principles).
